Data Appreciation

Data is how a modern world decides things — what to build, who to help, what to believe. In twelve Saturdays you learn to ask a question data can answer, collect it fairly, summarise and chart it honestly, read other people's charts with a detective's eye, and tell a true story with numbers. Free tools only: Google Sheets and CODAP.

How each Saturday works

  1. Recall — the session opens with last week's questions, before anything new.
  2. Understand — one idea, a picture of it and a worked example.
  3. Do — practise it on something real, with support and stretch.
  4. Show — every week ends in work that is assessed: a practical, an outside exercise, a written answer.

1What is data? It is all around you
Data is recorded facts about the world — from a market price to a football score — and it comes in kinds.
  • Say what data is, with three examples from your own week
  • Tell categorical data from numerical data
  • Tell discrete from continuous numbers

2Asking a good question: the investigation cycle
Every data investigation starts with a question you can answer with data, and follows five steps: Problem, Plan, Data, Analysis, Conclusion.
  • Turn a vague question into one data can answer
  • Name the five steps of PPDAC in order
  • Plan what to measure and how
Slides
3Collecting data fairly: surveys and samples
Who you ask decides what you find. A fair sample gives everyone a chance to be chosen; a fair question does not push an answer.
  • Explain why a sample is used
  • Spot a biased sample and a leading question
  • Write three fair survey questions
Slides
4Organising data: tidy tables in a spreadsheet
Tidy data has one row per thing observed and one column per thing measured — and then a spreadsheet can do the work.
  • Lay out data as a tidy table
  • Enter and format it in Google Sheets
  • Sort and filter it
Slides
5Summarising: mean, median, mode and range
One number can describe a whole list — but which one depends on the data, and one outlier can mislead the mean.
  • Work out mean, median, mode and range
  • Use AVERAGE, MEDIAN, MODE, MIN and MAX in Sheets
  • Choose the right average when there is an outlier
Slides
6Charts: choosing the right one
A chart answers a question. Pick the chart by the question: compare, change over time, part of a whole, spread, or relationship.
  • Match five questions to five chart types
  • Make a bar chart from tidy data in Sheets
  • Title and label a chart so it can be read alone
Slides
7Reading charts critically: how charts mislead
A chart can be accurate and still mislead — through a cut axis, a cherry-picked range, 3-D effects or a missing source.
  • Spot four common tricks in charts
  • Explain how a truncated axis exaggerates
  • Rewrite a misleading chart honestly
Slides
8Patterns, outliers and correlation
Scatter plots show whether two things go together — but going together is not the same as one causing the other.
  • Read a scatter plot: positive, negative or no relationship
  • Spot an outlier and decide what to do with it
  • Explain why correlation is not causation
Slides
9Data about us: privacy, consent and the law
Personal data belongs to the person it describes. Collect only what you need, ask first, keep it safe — it is the law in Nigeria.
  • Say what personal data is
  • Explain consent and collecting only what you need
  • Name Nigeria's data protection law and what it promises
Slides
10Bias in data and in AI
Data reflects who was counted. When people are missing from the data, decisions and AI trained on it fail them.
  • Explain how missing people make data biased
  • Show how an AI model learns bias from its training data
  • Suggest how to make data fairer
Slides
11Telling a story with data
One message, one clear chart, a headline that says it — and an honest note of what the data cannot tell you.
  • Write a headline that states a finding
  • Choose and clean one chart for one message
  • Present a finding in sixty seconds
Slides
12Project: your own data investigation
Run the whole investigation cycle on a question you care about — and defend it to a panel.
  • Carry out every step of PPDAC on your own question
  • Summarise and chart your data honestly
  • Defend your conclusion and its limits
Slides
