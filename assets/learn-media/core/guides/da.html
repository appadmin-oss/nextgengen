Data Appreciation

What every computer user should know about data, from the very basics to confident everyday use: how a computer stores data and how big it is, files and folders, saving and backing up, typing and accurate data entry, spreadsheets and formulas, sorting and charts, checking what you find online, keeping accounts and personal data safe, and sharing and working together. Free tools only: your own file manager, Google Sheets or LibreOffice Calc.

How each Saturday works

  1. Recall — the session opens with last week's questions, before anything new.
  2. Understand — one idea, a picture of it and a worked example.
  3. Do — practise it on something real, with support and stretch.
  4. Show — every week ends in work that is assessed: a practical, an outside exercise, a written answer.

A computer takes data in, processes it, stores it and gives it out — and it measures everything in bytes.
  • Explain input, process, output and storage with real examples
  • Say what a bit and a byte are
  • Read KB, MB, GB and TB, and check free space on a device

2Files, folders and good names
A file is one piece of saved work; folders keep them organised; a good name finds it again in six months.
  • Create, rename, move and delete files and folders
  • Read a file's extension and path
  • Name files so they sort and can be found

3Saving, storage and backups
Know where your work is saved — on the device, a flash drive or the cloud — and keep a backup before you need it.
  • Tell device storage, removable storage and cloud storage apart
  • Use Save and Save As correctly
  • Make a backup plan with the 3-2-1 rule
Slides
4The keyboard, shortcuts and entering data accurately
Fast is good; accurate is essential. Shortcuts save time, and consistent data entry saves everyone after you.
  • Use ten everyday keyboard shortcuts
  • Type with accuracy before speed
  • Enter data consistently: dates, names, numbers
Slides
5Spreadsheets I: cells, rows, columns and formatting
A spreadsheet is a grid for data: learn its parts, enter data cleanly and make it readable.
  • Name a cell by its column and row
  • Enter text, numbers and dates and format them
  • Make a table readable: headers, widths, number formats
Slides
6Spreadsheets II: formulas that do the work
Formulas calculate for you, and recalculate when the data changes: SUM, AVERAGE, MIN, MAX, COUNT — and fill down.
  • Write formulas with SUM, AVERAGE, MIN, MAX and COUNT
  • Fill a formula across or down
  • Explain relative and absolute references
Slides
7Sort, filter, find and tidy data
Answer questions from a long list in seconds — if the table is tidy and you sort the whole thing.
  • Sort a whole table by one column
  • Filter to show only some rows
  • Use Find and Replace, and keep data tidy
Slides
8Summaries and charts
Turn a column of numbers into one honest summary and one clear chart.
  • Find mean, median and mode, and choose the fair one
  • Choose a chart by the question
  • Make a labelled chart from a table
Slides
9Finding and checking information online
Search well, then check before you believe or share: who made it, when, and what do other trusted sources say?
  • Use search operators to find better results
  • Check a source with SIFT
  • Tell a fact from a claim, and treat AI answers as a starting point
Slides
10Staying safe: passwords, scams and personal data
Protect your accounts and other people's data: strong passwords, 2-step verification, spotting scams, and the law.
  • Make a strong passphrase and turn on 2-step verification
  • Spot a phishing message
  • Say what personal data is and what Nigeria's data law protects
Slides
11Sharing files and working together
Send the right file type, share with the right access, and work on one file together without losing anything.
  • Choose a file type for the job (PDF, DOCX, XLSX, CSV, JPG, PNG)
  • Share with viewer, commenter or editor access
  • Use comments and version history

Use everything from the term again on one real job: collect, organise, calculate, chart, share safely and back up.
  • Carry out a complete data task on your own
  • Use files, formulas, sorting, a chart and safe sharing together
