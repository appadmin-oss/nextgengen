Data Appreciation · Week 11 · Idea 1

Understand it

A data story has four parts:

  1. The headline says the finding: "Most members travel under 30 minutes" — not "Travel survey results".
  2. One chart that shows it, with nothing extra: no 3-D, no unnecessary colours, the important bar highlighted.
  3. The numbers that matter, in plain words: "31 of 40 members (78%)".
  4. The limits: who was asked, how many, and what the data cannot say.

People remember stories, not tables. But a story must be true to the data — the limits are part of the story, not small print.

Key words

Data Appreciation · Week 11 · Practice

Now you do it

  1. Write three headlines for your survey data
  2. Remove everything from your chart that does not help the message
  3. Present it to a partner in one minute

Need a hand? Fill the frame: "[Number] of [who] [finding]. This matters because …"

Done early? Find a data story in the news and judge it with the four parts.
