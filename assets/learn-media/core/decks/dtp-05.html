Desktop Publishing · Week 5 · Idea 1

Understand it

A grid divides the page into columns with gaps (gutters). Everything snaps to the columns, so the page looks orderly even with many parts. A headline might span three columns, a photo two, a sidebar one.

Four principles to check every page:

  • Contrast — make different things look very different (headline vs body).
  • Repetition — repeat fonts, colours and shapes so the page feels like one thing.
  • Alignment — every item lines up with something; nothing is placed "by eye".
  • Proximity — put related things close together and unrelated things apart.

Desktop Publishing · Week 5 · Practice

Now you do it

  1. Draw a 3-column grid on an A4 page
  2. Place a headline, photo and two stories on it
  3. Critique a classmate's page with the four principles

Need a hand? Turn on the grid or guides in your tool and snap every box to it.

Done early? Lay out the same content on a 2-column and a 4-column grid; say which works better and why.

Desktop Publishing · Week 5 · Assessment

How you will be assessed

  • Which principle groups a photo with its own caption, away from other stories?
  • Lay out an A4 page on a 3-column grid with a headline, a photo and two short stories, and label where each principle is used.

Your work is marked against the rubric, and you may be asked to explain your choices. Show your own thinking.
