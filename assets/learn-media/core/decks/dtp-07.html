Desktop Publishing · Week 7 · Idea 1

Understand it

  • Resolution: an image is made of pixels. Screens need about 72–150 pixels per inch; print needs about 300. A small image from a website looks fine on screen and blurry on paper. Never stretch an image bigger than it is.
  • Cropping removes what does not help: cut to the subject, keep eyes on the upper third, leave room in the direction a person is looking.
  • Rights: an image online is NOT free to use. Use your own photos, images with a licence that allows it (Creative Commons, Unsplash), and credit them. Ask permission before using a photo of a person — especially a child.
  • Alt text: on screen, describe the image for people who cannot see it.
  • AI-made images: if you generate a picture with an AI tool, check the tool's terms allow your use, say it was AI-made in the credit, look closely for errors (hands, text, signs), and never make a fake photo of a real person.

Desktop Publishing · Week 7 · Assessment

How you will be assessed

  • A 600 × 400 pixel logo looks sharp on screen. Printed at A4 width, it looks blurry. Why?
  • You want to use a photo you found on Google Images in your newsletter. What should you check, and what must you add?
  • Show one photo cropped three ways and the version you chose, with a reason.

Your work is marked against the rubric, and you may be asked to explain your choices. Show your own thinking.
