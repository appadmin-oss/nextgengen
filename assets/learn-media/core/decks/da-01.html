Data Appreciation · Week 1 · Idea 1

Understand it

Everything a computer handles is data: the letters you type, a photo, a song, your marks, a location.

Every device does four things with it:

  1. Input — data comes in (keyboard, touchscreen, camera, microphone).
  2. Process — the processor works on it (resizes the photo, adds the numbers).
  3. Output — it comes back out (screen, speaker, printer).
  4. Storage — it is kept for later (phone storage, a hard drive, a flash drive, the cloud).
Data Appreciation · Week 1 · Idea 2

Understand it

Inside, all of it is stored as bits — 0s and 1s. Eight bits make a byte, about one letter. Bigger amounts go up in steps of about a thousand: KB (kilobyte), MB (megabyte), GB (gigabyte), TB (terabyte). A text message is a few KB; a phone photo 2–5 MB; an hour of video around 1 GB.

Why it matters: when a phone says "storage full", when a file is "too large to attach", or when a data bundle runs out, it is these units you are reading.

Data Appreciation · Week 1 · Key words

Key words

Data
facts, words, sounds or pictures a computer can store and process
Bit
a single 0 or 1
Byte
8 bits — about one character
Storage
where data is kept when the device is off
